# Prickly Pear Care Brand Rules

## Brand identity

**Consumer brand:** Prickly Pear Care  
**Legal entity:** Prickly Pear Primary Care PLLC  
**Tagline:** Care that grows with you.  
**Care philosophy:** Patient-led care  
**Founder:** Jennifer Carlile, MSN, FNP-BC, Founder, CEO & Family Nurse Practitioner

The website should feel unmistakably like Jennifer's practice, not a generic healthcare template.

## Voice

Use language that is:

- warm without becoming casual about medicine,
- clear before clever,
- confident without overpromising,
- local without becoming Southwestern-themed,
- human without sounding sentimental,
- patient-led without implying that clinical judgment is absent.

Preferred phrasing includes:

- Patient-led care
- Your voice shapes the plan
- Clear choices and practical next steps
- Care that grows with you
- Rooted in Marana
- Direct Primary Care

Avoid defaulting to vague phrases such as "whole-person care," "compassionate care," "concierge experience," "premium healthcare," or "revolutionizing healthcare" unless the surrounding copy explains specifically what they mean.

## Visual direction

### Palette

Use the established olive/green, berry/magenta, warm cream, and charcoal system already present in the site. New shades must support the existing system rather than create a second palette.

### Photography

Priority order:

1. Real, approved photography of Jennifer.
2. Real, licensed Marana/Sonoran Desert photography.
3. Real office photography once the location exists.
4. AI-generated environmental or conceptual imagery only when it cannot misrepresent the real practice.

Never fabricate a patient testimonial, real office, procedure, clinical result, credential, or service using AI imagery.

Jennifer's likeness should not be regenerated for production assets when an approved real photograph can be used. If temporary AI concept imagery is used, it must not quietly become the canonical identity asset.

### Logo

The canonical consumer wording is **Prickly Pear Care**. Do not regenerate, redraw, or approximate the official logo in an image model. Use the actual supplied asset.

Do not display "Primary Care" as part of the consumer wordmark unless Jennifer explicitly changes the brand decision.

### Icons

Use Lucide React for interface icons. Keep stroke style and sizing consistent. Custom SVG is appropriate for the cactus/brand motif.

Emojis may be used sparingly in social/editorial content. They should not be used as primary navigation, form, clinical-status, alert, or booking icons.

## Motion language

Motion should communicate **growth, warmth, clarity, continuity, and response**.

Approved primitives:

- **Bloom:** subtle opacity plus upward reveal.
- **Grow:** line or progress element expanding from its origin.
- **Settle:** large image or hero element resolving gently into position.
- **Respond:** subtle button/card feedback.
- **Flow:** restrained progress or care-journey movement.

Rules:

- Motion for React is the default runtime.
- Prefer opacity and transform.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` through MotionConfig and CSS fallbacks.
- No scroll hijacking.
- No motion required to read, navigate, book, or understand a service.
- Avoid large parallax on mobile.
- Avoid looping decorative animation near clinical or pricing content.
- Do not mix Motion, Anime.js, GSAP, and Lottie unless each solves a distinct documented problem.

## UI character

The interface should feel editorial and intentional rather than dashboard-like or card-heavy.

Use cards when they help compare, choose, or group information. Do not turn every paragraph into a rounded card.

Use generous whitespace, readable typography, clear section hierarchy, authentic photography, restrained linework, and a small number of strong visual moments.

## Patient-led care consistency

"Patient-led care" is the approved language for the care philosophy. When describing the experience:

- the patient brings priorities, concerns, and goals,
- Jennifer brings clinical expertise, context, options, and recommendations,
- decisions are shared and clearly explained,
- the patient leaves understanding the next step.

Do not accidentally rewrite this concept into "provider-led care," "relationship-centered care," "patient-first care," or another substitute as the primary brand phrase. Those ideas may appear descriptively, but the canonical phrase remains **Patient-led care**.

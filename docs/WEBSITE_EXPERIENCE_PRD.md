# Prickly Pear Care Website Experience PRD

## Purpose

This document explains how the website should feel to a patient, what we are trying to accomplish, and what we still need to confirm before launch.

The goal is simple: someone should be able to visit the site and quickly understand who Jennifer is, what Direct Primary Care means, what "Patient-led care" looks like in real life, and what to do next.

This is not about adding more features for the sake of having more features. It is about making the website feel like the care experience Prickly Pear Care wants to provide: warm, clear, personal, local, trustworthy, and easy to navigate.

## The north star

**Patient-led care. Care that grows with you.**

The website should make that promise understandable without overpromising anything Jennifer has not finalized yet.

A patient should leave the site thinking:

1. I understand who Jennifer is and why I would trust her.
2. I understand the basic idea behind DPC.
3. I understand how patient-led care is different from feeling processed by a system.
4. I know what is available now and what is only planned for later.
5. I know exactly what to do if I want to stay connected.

## What is already working

The current website has a strong foundation:

- Jennifer is positioned as the clinical and personal trust anchor.
- The public-facing brand is Prickly Pear Care.
- "Patient-led care" is the approved care philosophy and stays consistent throughout the experience.
- "Care that grows with you." is the main brand line.
- The visual identity feels tied to Marana and the Sonoran Desert without becoming a desert-themed template.
- DPC is presented as the launch focus.
- Weight management, hormone support, and other future programs are clearly described as planned, not currently available.
- Motion is restrained and supports the story rather than distracting from it.
- The site avoids inventing pricing, access guarantees, or membership benefits that Jennifer has not approved.

## Who the website needs to serve

### 1. Someone who already knows Jennifer

Their question is not "Who is this provider?"

Their question is "How do I follow her to the new practice?"

They should be able to land on the homepage and reach the Waitlist almost immediately.

### 2. A new local patient

They need to know:

- who Jennifer is,
- how long she has practiced,
- how long she has served Marana and Northwest Tucson,
- what kind of experience she is trying to create,
- what DPC means,
- and whether the practice feels credible and established enough to trust.

### 3. Someone who has never heard of DPC

They may assume it is concierge medicine, insurance, a subscription that replaces insurance, or something expensive and complicated.

The website should explain DPC clearly without making promises before the final membership is approved.

### 4. Someone interested in future services

They may care about weight management, hormone support, general primary care, preventive care, or future programs.

The site should allow them to express interest without implying those services are already available.

## Changes included in the conversion pass

### 1. One clear launch action

The conversion CTA is now consistently:

**Join the Waitlist**

Other labels such as "Join the interest list," "Stay in the loop," and "Request a visit" should not compete with it before live scheduling opens.

Secondary actions can still be:

- Explore Direct Primary Care
- Meet Jennifer
- Ask a Question

### 2. Jennifer's trust proof moves closer to the top

The homepage now surfaces the strongest proof earlier:

- 13+ years practicing as a Family Nurse Practitioner
- 10+ years serving Marana and Northwest Tucson
- FNP-BC
- founder-led care with Jennifer Carlile

The purpose is not to turn her experience into flashy statistics. It is to answer the patient's quiet question: "Is this a brand-new provider, or someone who already knows this community?"

### 3. Patient-led care now naturally leads into DPC

After the patient-led care journey, the website now asks the next logical question:

**How does this fit into Direct Primary Care?**

This makes the homepage feel like one conversation instead of several unrelated sections.

### 4. The DPC page now answers "Why would I care?"

The site now compares common frustrations people may recognize with what Prickly Pear Care is intentionally trying to build.

Examples:

- care can feel fragmented -> build continuity
- next steps can feel unclear -> explain choices and what happens next
- the system can drive the visit -> patient priorities start the conversation
- the model can be hard to compare -> publish membership terms before enrollment

The language is intentionally careful. We are not claiming every traditional practice is bad.

### 5. The DPC page brings Jennifer back into the story

The page now makes a key idea explicit:

**DPC is the structure. Jennifer is the relationship.**

The website should never make the model feel more important than the provider-patient relationship it is meant to support.

### 6. The Waitlist is easier to complete

The required information is reduced to the essentials:

- first name
- last name
- email
- consent

Phone, ZIP code, care format, timing, and service interests are optional planning questions.

The message to patients is: "Your name and email are enough. If you want to help Jennifer understand what the community needs, you can answer a few more questions."

### 7. Optional questions become useful business insight

The Waitlist can now capture interest in:

- Direct Primary Care
- general primary care updates
- future weight management updates
- future hormone support updates
- other future services or "not sure yet"

These answers are explicitly framed as interest, not currently available services.

Over time, this can help Jennifer make better decisions about what patients are actually asking for.

### 8. The post-Waitlist experience keeps the relationship going

The fallback confirmation state now gives people useful next steps:

- Meet Jennifer
- Understand DPC

Once the final secure form workflow is selected, the true confirmation page should carry the same idea.

### 9. Navigation is simpler

The main navigation now prioritizes the decisions a prospective patient is most likely to make:

- Direct Primary Care
- Meet Jennifer
- Care & Services
- New Patients
- About
- Patient Portal
- Join the Waitlist

Resources remain available in the footer until there is enough published content to justify a primary navigation position.

## What the site should not claim until Jennifer confirms it

The website should not present any of the following as final until Jennifer approves them:

- DPC membership price
- what is included in membership
- what is billed separately
- same-day or next-day availability
- unlimited visits
- direct text access
- response-time promises
- after-hours access
- included labs or medications
- telehealth availability
- exact office location
- office hours
- patient age ranges
- cancellation or renewal terms
- weight management launch details
- hormone support launch details
- future service eligibility
- EHR vendor or portal features

## What we still need from Jennifer

Before final launch, Jennifer should confirm:

### DPC

- Monthly or annual membership pricing
- Enrollment fees, if any
- What the membership includes
- What is not included
- Age ranges or eligibility rules
- Lab, imaging, medication, and specialist workflow
- Insurance relationship
- Cancellation and renewal rules
- Communication methods and response expectations

### Practice operations

- Opening date or opening window
- Office address
- Office hours
- Phone number
- Telehealth decision
- Secure scheduling workflow
- EHR and patient portal vendor

### Future services

For weight management, hormone support, or any other future program:

- Is the service approved to advertise?
- When will it launch?
- Who is eligible?
- Is it part of DPC or separate?
- How is it priced?
- What can the website safely and accurately say about it?

### Brand and content

- Final approval of the Jennifer biography
- Final approval of the "Patient-led care" language
- Final approval of real photography for homepage and provider pages
- Approved patient reviews or testimonials, if they will be used
- Social media links

## How we will know the site is working

Once analytics are implemented in a privacy-conscious way, the most useful measures are:

- Homepage -> DPC click rate
- Homepage -> Meet Jennifer click rate
- Homepage -> Waitlist click rate
- DPC page -> Waitlist click rate
- Waitlist start rate
- Waitlist completion rate
- Mobile vs desktop completion rate
- Which optional service interests are selected
- Which pages people visit before joining the Waitlist

The goal is not to chase clicks. The goal is to find where people become confused, lose trust, or stop before taking the next step.

## Launch quality gate

The website should not be considered fully launch-ready until all of the following are true:

- Jennifer approves the business facts and clinical/service wording.
- The secure Waitlist workflow is finalized.
- The site builds successfully with no lint or build errors.
- Phone, tablet, laptop, and large desktop layouts have been visually reviewed.
- Reduced-motion behavior has been tested.
- Keyboard navigation and form focus states have been checked.
- Core images are optimized and do not create layout shift.
- Legal/privacy language has received appropriate review.
- Any analytics implementation has been reviewed for the healthcare/privacy context.
- DPC pricing and membership details are accurate before enrollment opens.

## Decision summary for Jennifer

The website is being designed around three ideas:

1. **You are the reason people trust the practice.** The website should make your experience and relationship with the community visible early.
2. **Patient-led care is the philosophy.** The patient brings their story, goals, and priorities. You bring clinical expertise, recommendations, context, and options. The next step is clear and shared.
3. **DPC is the launch model, not the entire identity of the business.** Prickly Pear Care can grow into additional services later without needing a new brand or a new website.

If those three ideas feel right, the website strategy is aligned with the practice we are trying to build.
